Chinchilla Facts

♡ ❀ ♡

Where Do Chinchillas Come From?

Chinchillas are native to the Andes Mountains of South America, where they live in rocky, mountainous environments at high elevations.

Wild chinchillas are naturally adapted to cooler, relatively dry climates and spend much of their time hiding among rocks and crevices.

How Long Do Chinchillas Live?

With proper care, chinchillas can live for many years. A well-cared-for chinchilla can commonly reach 15–20 years of age, making them a long-term commitment.

Their long lifespan is one of the many reasons it is important to carefully consider whether a chinchilla is the right companion for your household.

Those Amazing Fur Coats

Chinchillas have one of the densest fur coats of any land mammal. Their incredibly soft fur helps protect them from the cold temperatures of their natural mountain environment.

Because their fur is so dense, chinchillas should never be bathed in water. Moisture can become trapped close to the skin and cause problems with their coat and health.

Why Do Chinchillas Take Dust Baths?

Instead of bathing in water, chinchillas clean their fur by rolling around in specially formulated volcanic dust.

The fine dust reaches deep into their dense fur and absorbs excess oils and moisture, helping keep their coat clean, soft and healthy.

Chinchillas Are Crepuscular

Chinchillas are generally most active during the evening and early morning hours. They are often described as crepuscular, meaning their activity tends to increase around dawn and dusk.

Individual chinchillas can still have different routines depending on their environment and personality.

They Are Excellent Jumpers

Chinchillas are incredibly agile and can jump surprisingly high. Their strong hind legs allow them to move quickly between rocks and different levels of their environment.

This is one reason chinchillas benefit from safe, appropriately designed enclosures that allow them to climb, jump and explore.

What Do Chinchillas Eat?

Chinchillas are herbivores and need a high-fibre diet consisting of Timothy hay, appropriate chinchilla pellets and fresh water.

Treats are not needed or necessary. Instead, offer safe chew items such as apple sticks or other chinchilla-safe wood toys.

Chinchilla Teeth Never Stop Growing

Like many rodents, chinchillas have continuously growing teeth. Their teeth naturally wear down through chewing and eating fibrous foods.

Providing appropriate safe chewing materials and a suitable diet is therefore an important part of chinchilla care.

They Are Very Social Animals

Chinchillas are naturally social animals and often enjoy the company of other compatible chinchillas.

However, introductions should always be handled carefully and individual personalities and compatibility need to be taken into consideration.

They Can't Sweat Like We Do

Chinchillas have very limited ability to cool themselves because they don't have functional sweat glands like humans. Their incredibly dense fur also makes overheating a serious concern.
